# Capstone lab

## In plain words

**Goal:** Run the full context loop on one real job you own — not just read about it.

**Do this:** Pick a finishable workflow. Configure every stop before you generate. Run once with a timer. Verify, measure, and write a look-back.

**You'll know it worked when:** You ship one artifact with a verify pass, one metric, and one team default you will reuse next month.

## What this is

The capstone is practice on a **real job you own**. You set up the loop. You run it. You measure it. You check it. You keep what worked.

Reading alone does not change Monday. This lab installs the habit by running the full loop on one workflow end to end.

Think of it as a dress rehearsal before the real show — same steps, lower stakes, timed.

## Why it matters

Nina read every module but never ran the loop on her own work. On Monday she opened a blank chat and skipped research, verify, and handoffs. Quality dropped. She felt like the course "did not stick."

One measured capstone run changed that. Nina picked a weekly status note. She configured BRIEF.md and wall rules. She ran once, timed it, and wrote a look-back. The next Monday she reused the pack.

The capstone turns the map into muscle memory.

## Big ideas

### Pick a real workflow

Choose something you do often:

- Weekly status note for your manager
- Research brief before a meeting
- Support reply from policy pages
- Small code change with a test

Make it **finishable in under 90 focused minutes**. One outcome only. If it needs a week, cut scope.

**Design rule:** If you cannot name the artifact you will ship, the workflow is too big.

### Configure before you generate

Do not draft first. Set up the spine in plain words. Walk through every stop before you generate anything:

1. **Research pack** — Update **BRIEF.md** and **SOURCES.md**. Fact-finding before you start.
2. **Wall rules** — Short standing instructions on the classroom **wall** for tone and limits.
3. **Playbook** — One **recipe** card for this workflow (not the whole wall).
4. **Tools** — Small **tool** allow list. Least apps needed.
5. **Retrieval** — Plan how you will look up binder pages at answer time.
6. **Compaction** — Plan your handoff template if the chat runs long.
7. **Memory** — Decide what stays in binder notes vs product **memory**. Prune stale stickies.
8. **Delegation** — Split or not? If yes, write specialist briefs and name a merge owner.
9. **Human craft** — Write your spec: outcome, audience, limits, sources, done checks.
10. **Verify** — Write done checks and safety gates before you run.

Only after step 10 do you generate the artifact.

### Run and measure

Run the workflow once with a timer.

Track at least one metric:

- Minutes to first usable draft
- Number of revision rounds
- Errors caught in verify
- Facts that needed a source fix

Compare to your old way. Write notes — even three bullets help.

### Look-back and keep defaults

After the run, write a short look-back:

- What improved vs your old habit?
- Which loop stop failed first (if anything did)?
- What becomes a **team default** — a rule, checklist, or pack habit?

Share one default with a teammate. Revisit in 30 days and prune what did not stick.

### A simple walkthrough

You capstone a support reply workflow.

1. **Scope** — One reply type: refund requests. Artifact: reply draft. Time box: 60 minutes.
2. **Configure** — BRIEF.md goal line. SOURCES.md policy link. Wall rule: plain tone. Recipe card: reply spec. Two tools max. Verify checklist ready.
3. **Run** — Spec in first message. Retrieve policy page. Draft once. No side jobs.
4. **Verify** — Done checks, source quotes, no secrets, no extra promises.
5. **Measure** — Old way: 25 min and two guess errors. New way: 18 min, zero guess errors.
6. **Look-back** — Keep SOURCES.md link in every refund ticket. Share checklist with desk mate.

If step 4 fails, fix the configure step — not the model brand.

## Where this sits in the loop

The capstone is **every stop on one real task** — research, wall, recipe, tools, retrieval, compaction, memory, delegation, craft, verify:

- Module 2: deep research (BRIEF.md and SOURCES.md first)
- Module 11: verify (check before you ship)
- Module 12: capstone lab (this lesson — run it all)

When something breaks, ask: **Which habit was missing?**

## What goes wrong if you skip it

- Learning stays on the page. Monday habits do not move.
- You configure once and never measure — no proof it helped.
- Scope too big — you abort and blame the method.
- You skip verify to save time — errors ship anyway.
- Pack files stay empty — wall rules encode guesses again.
- No look-back — teammates never inherit your defaults.

## Where this shows up in tools

Use whatever helper you already use. If you plug in tools through **MCP**, remember MCP means Model Context Protocol — a shared app menu. Keep that list tiny for the lab.

| Idea | ChatGPT | Claude | Cursor | Copilot-style |

Your daily helper app is the stage. The loop is the script.

## Tips

1. **Timebox setup to 25 minutes**, then run — do not perfect the pack first.
2. **Keep before/after notes** — one metric beats a vague "felt better."
3. **Share one default** with a teammate the same week.
4. **Revisit in 30 days** — prune habits that did not stick.
5. **Cut scope ruthlessly** — one artifact, one workflow, one hour.
6. **Run verify out loud** — say each done check before you ship.

The capstone is not a test of the AI. It is a test of your loop habits on real work.
