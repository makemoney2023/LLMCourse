# Standing playbooks

## In plain words

**Goal:** Keep always-on wall rules short. Save step-by-step workflows as recipe cards you pull out only when needed.

**Do this:** Sort each guideline into wall or recipe. Write cards with trigger phrases, numbered steps, and testable done checks. Point cards at BRIEF.md and SOURCES.md for facts. Never paste facts onto the wall.

**You'll know it worked:** A teammate opens the right card, runs one real job, and passes every done check without your chat history.

## What this is

You pull out a recipe card only when you're cooking that dish — status email, incident note, PR write-up. That's a **playbook**. **Always-on rules** work differently: they stay on the classroom wall and apply to most work in a space.

This lesson teaches you to split **wall** from **recipe card**. The wall stays short and stable. Playbooks hold steps, triggers, and "done when" checks for named jobs.

Facts from your research pack live in BRIEF.md and SOURCES.md. Behavior defaults live on the wall. Workflow steps live on recipe cards. Mixing all three on the wall fills the desk and confuses the helper.

## Why it matters

One team pasted a great prompt into every chat. "Write the board update like this…" with twelve steps. The desk filled with unused rules. Quality dropped mid-thread because tool results had no room.

Another team wrote nothing down. Every chat started from zero. Tone drifted. Done checks changed person to person.

You need both: a **short wall** and **good recipe cards**. The wall says who you are and what never to do. The card says how to run this one workflow today.

## Big ideas

### Always-on rules (the wall)

Keep them short, stable, and true most of the time.

Examples: "Do not share secrets." "Ask if a fact is missing." "Use BRIEF.md for product limits."

These sit next to wall instructions from the prior lesson. Many tools call them project rules or AGENTS.md lines. They load every turn whether you need them or not.

**Design rule:** If a line is true for almost every task, it stays on the wall. If not, move it to a recipe card.

### On-demand playbooks (recipe cards)

Use a recipe card for a special job with a clear start and end.

A good card includes:

- **Trigger phrases** — words that tell people when to open the card. Example: "/status", "draft board update."
- **Steps** — numbered actions in order.
- **Done checks** — testable lines anyone can verify. Example: "Includes date, owner, and one risk line."

Keep steps and done checks on the card — not on the wall. Pull the card in only when the task matches.

### Facts are not rules

Long competitor lists, API field tables, and pricing grids belong in SOURCES.md. Do not paste them into always-on rules. That burns desk space and goes stale fast.

Point playbooks at the binder: "Read pricing from SOURCES.md row 4 before drafting."

**Design rule:** Rules say what to do. Facts say what is true. Never confuse the two on the wall.

### A simple walkthrough

Your team sends a weekly status email. You already have BRIEF.md from research.

1. **Wall check** — Wall has tone, secrets rule, and "cite SOURCES.md for numbers." Nothing about status format.
2. **Write the card** — Trigger: "weekly status." Steps: gather wins, risks, asks. Done: under 200 words, dated, one link max.
3. **Test once** — Open a fresh chat. Paste the card. Run one real status. Check done lines.
4. **Store it** — Save as a skill, slash command, or saved prompt. Name it so others find it.
5. **Verify** — A teammate uses only the card plus BRIEF.md. They succeed without your chat history.

If step 5 fails, the card is missing a step or a done check. Fix the card, not the wall.

Together, shared rules and playbooks become workspace assets. Give each one an owner and review date.

## Where this sits in the loop

Playbooks sit between wall rules and tools. Module 3: system instructions give you always-on behavior. Module 5: tools and MCP add apps the helper may open during a card step.

Wall rules load every turn. Recipe cards enter only when the task matches. That keeps desk space for tool results and retrieved pages.

## What goes wrong if you skip it

- The desk fills with unused rules from pasted mega-prompts. Tool output gets squeezed out.
- Every chat reinvents the same workflow. Quality and tone drift across teammates.
- Facts get pasted onto the wall as rules. They go stale and nobody knows which line to trust.
- Playbooks have no trigger phrase. People forget they exist and rewrite from scratch.
- Done checks live only in someone's head. Verify becomes "looks fine I guess."

Names change. The split stays: short wall, named recipe cards, facts in the binder.

## Tips

1. **If a line is only for one workflow, make it a recipe card.** Do not put it on the wall.
2. **Keep wall rules under one short screen.** Everything on the wall loads every turn.
3. **Build playbooks from BRIEF.md constraints** when you can. Cards should match real limits.
4. **Name triggers clearly.** "/incident", "draft QBR" — people must know when to open the card.
5. **Write done checks a human can test.** "Includes owner name" beats "sounds professional."
6. **Review cards quarterly.** Delete cards nobody used in ninety days.
