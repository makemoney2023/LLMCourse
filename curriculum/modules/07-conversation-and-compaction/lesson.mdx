# Conversation and compaction

## In plain words

**Goal:** Keep one chat useful without old junk mixing into new work.

**Do this:** After big tool dumps, ask for ten bullets. Write a short handoff note when you start fresh. Choose continue or restart on purpose.

**You'll know:** A teammate could read your handoff alone and pick up the job. Old tool logs are not steering your next reply.

## What this is

Every chat is a working session. You ask. The helper replies. Apps dump results. Fixes pile up. All of it sits on the same **desk space** from Module 1.

**Compaction** is like writing a short note to clear the desk. You keep what matters. You drop the noise. A **handoff note** is that short note. You paste it into a fresh chat when you **restart**.

This lesson teaches when to keep going and when to start over. It also teaches what to save in a handoff.

## Why it matters

Maya ran one chat for three days. She fixed a report, then pivoted to a slide deck, then asked for code. Tool logs filled half the thread. Old refund numbers came back in the new draft. Maya blamed the model. The desk was full of stale papers.

A ten-line handoff would have fixed it. Maya could have restarted with Goal, Done checks, and Sources only. Quality would have jumped back up.

Long threads feel productive. They often hide bad old results. A clean handoff beats one more confused reply.

## Big ideas

### What piles up on the desk

Every turn adds papers:

- Your goals and corrections
- Helper drafts you rejected or accepted
- **Tool dumps** — often the biggest stack
- Auto summaries the app may insert
- Standing rules and attached files from earlier modules

When the desk fills, the model may drop early turns. It may also treat old tool output as still true.

**Design rule:** After a big tool dump, ask for ten bullets. Then drop the raw log from the active thread.

### Good handoff notes

A handoff is a recipe card for the next session. Keep these parts:

- **Goal** — one clear job
- **Done checks** — how you will know it is finished
- **Locked choices** — decisions you will not reopen
- **Sources** — links or file names (a one-page project summary (BRIEF.md), a link list (SOURCES.md))
- **Do not** — limits and out-of-scope lines
- **Open questions** — what still needs a human call

Drop long rants, rejected drafts, and raw logs. Your teammate should continue from the note alone.

### Continue vs restart

**Continue** when you are polishing the same job. Same goal. Same sources. One small fix left.

**Restart** when:

- The topic shifted to a new job
- Sources changed or facts were wrong
- The same mistake keeps coming back
- Tool output is stale but still on the desk
- Quality drifted and you cannot find why

Paste the handoff — not the whole old chat.

### A simple walkthrough

You spent two hours drafting a customer FAQ from SOURCES.md.

1. **Check the desk** — Is the thread mostly FAQ work? If yes, continue for polish.
2. **Big tool dump** — A search returned forty pages. Ask for ten bullets. Remove the raw dump from focus.
3. **Topic shift** — Your boss asks for a sales email in the same chat. That is a new job. Write a handoff. Restart.
4. **Handoff** — Goal: FAQ for refunds. Done: five questions, cites SOURCES.md. Locked: friendly tone. Do not: legal advice.
5. **Fresh chat** — Paste the handoff. Attach BRIEF.md if needed. Run again.

If step 5 still drifts, your handoff missed a locked choice or a do-not line.

## Where this sits in the loop

Compaction is how you manage working papers on the desk across turns. It sits after lookup and before sticky notes for later.

- Module 6: retrieval (look up in a binder before you answer)
- Module 7: compaction (short note to clear desk — this lesson)
- Module 8: memory (sticky notes for later)

Compaction does not replace research or verify. It keeps the desk usable so those steps still work.

## What goes wrong if you skip it

- Stale tool output wins over fresh facts.
- Rejected ideas come back in new drafts.
- The desk overflows. Early goals get cut silently.
- You keep "continuing" when you should restart.
- Handoffs are too long. They become a second cluttered desk.
- Teams blame the model when the thread is the problem.

## Where this shows up in tools

| Idea | ChatGPT | Claude | Cursor | Copilot-style |
|------|---------|--------|--------|---------------|
| Long threads | Chat history | Project threads | Agent transcripts | Long PR threads |
| Compaction | Summarize chat | Thread summaries | Handoff in new chat | Thread recap |
| Restart | New chat + paste | New project chat | New agent session | Fresh session |
| Handoff format | User-written note | User-written note | AGENTS.md snippet | Ticket brief |

Names change. The habit stays the same: shrink the past, keep the spine.

## Tips

1. **After every big tool dump**, ask for ten bullets, then drop the raw log.
2. **Keep a living "decisions" list** inside your handoff template.
3. **Restart with Goal / Done / Sources / Do not** — four lines minimum.
4. **One job per chat** when you can. Mixed jobs pollute the desk fast.
5. **Say "continue or restart?"** out loud when quality drifts.
6. **Date your handoffs** so you know which facts were current.

You are not deleting work. You are filing it so the next turn starts clean.
