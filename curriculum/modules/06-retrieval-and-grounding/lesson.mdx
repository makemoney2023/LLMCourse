# Retrieval and grounding

## What this is

**Retrieval** is like looking something up in a binder before you answer. You bring the right page to the desk — a file, a link, a search hit — then you read it.

**Grounding** means the answer rests on those pages, not on guesses. Fluent words are not the same as true words. If the job needs your policy or your **BRIEF.md**, the helper must see that page first.

**Cite then answer** is the habit: name the source, quote or point to it, then write the reply. If the page is missing, say you do not know. Do not invent.

## Why it matters

A support lead asked for refund rules. The helper wrote a clear, confident policy paragraph. It sounded perfect. None of it matched the real handbook. A customer almost got the wrong deadline.

The wall rules said "be helpful." Nobody attached the handbook page. Retrieval was skipped. Grounding failed. Verify caught it — but only because a human read the draft before send.

When facts matter, retrieve first. Ground second. Answer third.

## Big ideas

### Retrieve, then answer

Use this order every time facts matter:

1. **Name the question** — What exact fact do you need?
2. **Bring the page to the desk** — Attach BRIEF.md, a SOURCES.md row, or one good doc section.
3. **Quote or point** — Pull the line that holds the answer.
4. **Answer from that page** — No extra facts from memory.
5. **Stop if missing** — Say "not in the sources" instead of guessing.

**Design rule:** If you cannot point to a line, the answer is not grounded yet.

### Use your research pack

From Module 2, keep **BRIEF.md** and **SOURCES.md** handy. They are your team's binder.

Attach short parts — one section, one row, one glossary entry. Do not dump every raw crawl onto the desk every turn. That buries the question and wastes desk space.

When SOURCES.md has the link, retrieve the smallest slice that answers today’s ask.

### Standing rules are not evidence

Module 3 wall rules steer behavior. They are not your fact binder. A line that says "we never discount" is a rule — not proof of this quarter's promo.

Module 8 **sticky notes** (memory) are also not today's source of truth. Memory helps across sessions. Retrieval pulls the page for **this** answer.

**Design rule:** Behavior on the wall. Facts in the binder. Do not mix them at answer time.

### Grounding vs sounding smart

Models are good at smooth prose. That is not grounding. Grounding means a teammate can follow your cite back to the source and find the same fact.

Ask for quotes when stakes are high. "Quote the refund window from handbook section 4, then draft the reply."

### A simple walkthrough

You answer: "Can plan Basic export CSV this month?"

1. **Retrieve** — Attach BRIEF.md limits section and the SOURCES.md row for the pricing page (dated last week).
2. **Quote** — Helper pulls: "Basic plan: no CSV export. Pro plan: CSV included."
3. **Answer** — "No. Basic cannot export CSV. Pro can. Source: pricing page, dated 2026-07-21."
4. **Verify** — You open the link in SOURCES.md. The line matches.
5. **If missing** — If the page changed, update SOURCES.md before you reuse the answer.

If step 4 fails, you skipped retrieve or used a stale file. Fix the binder, not the chat tone.

## Where this sits in the loop

Module 1 mapped the full context loop. Retrieval and grounding happen **when facts must be on the desk before the model writes**.

They follow tools and feed verify:

- Module 5: tools and MCP (read file, search, attach — how pages arrive)
- Module 6: retrieval and grounding (this lesson — cite then answer)
- Module 7: compaction (short note to clear desk after big retrieves)
- Module 8: memory (sticky notes — recall later, not proof now)
- Module 9: delegation (pass retrieved pages with the handoff)

Retrieval decides **what evidence** is on the desk. Tools (Module 5) are often **how** you fetch it. Verify (Module 11) checks that cites match real pages.

## What goes wrong if you skip it

- Confident wrong answers. Smooth prose hides missing proof.
- The desk gets buried under huge unused PDFs. The model never finds the right paragraph.
- Wall rules get treated as facts. Stale "always" lines replace live policy.
- Chat memory replaces SOURCES.md. Nobody can audit where a number came from.
- You verify tone but not cites. Errors ship because the draft "sounded right."

## Tips

1. **Prefer one good page over twenty random PDFs.** Small beats big on the desk.
2. **Say "quote the source"** when stakes are high. Tone tasks need less; number tasks need more.
3. **Refresh SOURCES.md when facts change.** Stale cites fail verify silently.
4. **Attach BRIEF.md sections**, not the whole research crawl, unless you truly need all of it.
5. **Separate retrieve steps in recipe cards** (Module 4). Make lookup explicit before draft steps.
6. **Verify cites, not vibes.** Open the link. Match the line. Then approve the send.
